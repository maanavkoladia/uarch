========================================================================
Main Memory Subsystem - Concise Design Overview
========================================================================

1. ROLE
-------
Back end of the storage hierarchy. Services DCache line fills,
ICache line fills, DCache writebacks, and DMA writes - all four
arrive at mem_TOP uniformly as a ld_req or st_req on the DTE
interface. Talks to the DTE (ld_req/st_req in, mem_Ready out) and
to the scheduler (writeBuf_V[NUM_BANK_GROUPS] back-pressure).
Receives the address bus and drives the data bus under DTE
permission.


2. GEOMETRY
-----------
Total memory: 32 KiB, 15-bit physical address.

  64 banks, organised three different ways:
    - 8 bank groups x 8 banks/group   (write routing)
    - 16 chips      x 4 banks/chip    (load routing / open-row)
  Each bank: 4 x sram32x32$ = 32 rows of 16 bytes.
  One row == one cache line.

Address layout:
  [14:10] row inside a chip (5 bits, 32 rows)
  [ 9: 6] chip number       (4 bits, 16 chips)
  [ 5: 4] bank within chip  (2 bits, 4 banks/chip)
  [ 3: 0] byte offset       (4 bits, 16B line)

Bank group is a different slice of the same address
(bankGroup = address[6:4]), so writes and loads naturally fan out
across the topology.


3. HIERARCHY
------------
mem_TOP
  +-- mem_bank x 64       (each owns its own FSM and 4 SRAM cells)
  +-- mem_controller      (chipTable, bankGroupTable, top-level FSM)

A 128-bit internal mem_bus carries a full 16B line out of one bank;
mem_TOP marshalls it onto the 32-bit external data bus over 4 beats
under DTE permission.


4. KEY DESIGN CHOICES AND THEIR PAYOFF
--------------------------------------
4.1 DDR5-style chip / bank-group / bank topology
    - Models real DRAM organisation, so the timing characteristics
      (row open, tRCD-equivalent, write latency) match what the
      pipeline will see in silicon-class behavior.
    - The two orthogonal slicings (chip-major for loads, bank-
      group-major for stores) keep concurrent loads and stores out
      of each other's way.

4.2 Open-row chipTable
    - Per-chip scoreboard of "what row is currently open and is it
      ready". A second load to the same row is a 1-cycle hit
      decision in the controller FSM, then the line walks out in
      4 beats - no precharge wait.
    - Stride-1 / streaming access patterns within a 32-row chip
      pay the row-opening cost once, then hit until the row is
      changed.

4.3 Bank-level pseudo-precharge FSM
    - Each bank has its own FSM that walks through LD_WAIT states
      after a row change, modelling row-access latency. Once it
      reaches "row ready", the bank stays ready until the row
      changes.
    - Because every bank does this on its own clock, banks across
      different chips can be opening different rows in parallel
      without any centralised scheduling.

4.4 Per-bank-group write buffer
    - The scheduler can issue a 16B write to a bank group even
      while the actual SRAM commit is still in progress: the
      controller absorbs the bus payload into a write buffer in 4
      beats, then hands it off to the targeted bank, then
      immediately returns to IDLE.
    - The bank's long ST_ADDR_WAIT + ST_WRITE_WAIT sequence does
      not hold up the controller or the bus. writeBuf_V to the
      scheduler is the back-pressure that prevents a second write
      to the same group before the first commits.

4.5 Layered bus permission
    - Three independent permission gates compose to drive the
      external data bus:
        controller decides which internal bank may drive mem_bus,
        the bank's FSM owns its OE,
        the DTE owns the per-slice data-bus permission.
    - This lets the same set of buses be shared across the whole
      memory subsystem and the rest of the chip with no internal
      arbitration logic.

4.6 Read/write mutual exclusion enforced by FSM
    - ld_req + st_req in the same cycle drops the controller into
      its synthesised ERROR state. The scheduler is responsible
      for never asserting both. Simplifies the controller and
      makes illegal traffic visible immediately in simulation.


5. PERFORMANCE SUMMARY
----------------------
  - Open-row hit (load whose row is the chipTable entry and the
    bank is precharged): one decision cycle in IDLE, then 4 bus
    beats = 5 cycles total to put a 16B line on the bus.
  - Closed-row miss: chipTable updates and the targeted chip's
    banks reset; controller spins in LD_MISS until the bank walks
    through 8 LD_WAIT states; then the same 4-beat drive.
  - Writes: 4-beat absorb into the bank-group write buffer (the
    controller is free to take a new request after that), then a
    long bank-internal commit that is invisible to the bus.
  - Bank-group parallelism: 8 independent bank-group write
    buffers, so up to 8 stores can be in-flight against memory
    simultaneously without bus contention.
  - Chip-level parallelism: 16 chips can each hold a different
    open row at once; the cost of a row miss is paid by one
    chip's banks, not by the whole memory.
